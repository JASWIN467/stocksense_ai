Academic Methodology & Limitations

Observational vs. Causal Interpretation

Historical variations across seasons and promotional periods reflect empirical observational averages. In retail data science, observing higher sales during summer or promotional dates indicates statistical correlation but does not constitute controlled causal proof without randomized A/B experimentation.

Data Span Limitations

The dataset encompasses exactly 731 chronological days (January 1, 2022 to January 1, 2024). Consequently, annual seasonal transitions are observed across two full cycles, which is sufficient for non-parametric regression but requires continuous monitoring in live deployment.

Data Leakage Audit & Academic Methodology Defense

1. Inverted Task & Data Leakage Resolution

In raw Kaggle formulations and early student projects, models were trained to predict an artificial column named Demand Forecast using contemporaneous Units Sold. This produced an artificial R² = 0.9937 because same-day sales is an outcome variable. StockSense AI solved this by designating actual future sales (Units Sold) as the true prediction target, eliminating contemporaneous leakage entirely.

2. Strict Temporal Validation vs Random Shuffling

Random train-test splits shuffle future observations into the past. StockSense AI strictly split data chronologically at August 8, 2023: 58,400 training observations (80%) vs. 14,700 testing observations (20%).

3. Transparent Feature Importance vs. Causal Claims

Feature importance scores identify which variables the decision trees split on most frequently to reduce mean squared error. They do not constitute guaranteed directional or causal effects.
